Title: Bridging the Third- and Fourth-Party Visibility Gap: A Continuous Monitoring Framework for Multi-Cloud Ecosystems in Indian Enterprises — Empirical Evaluation of Exposure Time Reduction Using SBOM Exchange, Security Ratings, and DPDPA-Aligned Contractual Audits.
Research Question: To what extent does a structured continuous third-party risk monitoring framework — integrating periodic SBOM exchange, vendor security ratings, and DPDPA-triggered audits (e.g., on rating drops or breach notification timelines) — reduce mean-time-to-detect (MTTD) and overall exposure for third- and fourth-party risks in hybrid/multi-cloud environments, compared to traditional periodic assessments? (Include analysis of false positive rates.)
Core Problem & Gap: In 2026, a clear third-party risk maturity gap exists: many organizations report confidence in their TPRM programs, yet only about 15% of CISOs claim full visibility into their supply chains, with even weaker oversight of fourth-party (sub-contractors and vendors-of-vendors) risks. Visibility is often limited to direct third parties, while breaches continue to rise through cloud dependencies and outsourcing. In India, DPDPA adds pressure because Data Fiduciaries bear full liability for processor failures, including timely breach notifications (with heavy fines possible). Most existing studies focus on Western contexts or point-in-time assessments; few provide empirical comparisons of continuous vs. periodic approaches with measurable metrics like MTTD and false positives in multi-cloud settings.
Methodology:
·	Design a continuous monitoring framework that uses SBOM generation/exchange (tools like Syft + Grype), public/commercial security ratings, and triggers based on DPDPA breach notification rules or rating drops to initiate audits.
·	Lab simulation: Set up Kubernetes (minikube or kind) with sample pods/containers representing multi-cloud-like environments. Inject known vulnerabilities. Compare periodic (e.g., quarterly) vs. continuous monitoring on metrics: MTTD, false positive rate, remediation effort.
·	Optional: Small survey or anonymized case data from Indian enterprises for real-world validation.
·	Simple scripts (Python/Bash): syft <image> --json > sbom.json; grype sbom:sbom.json.
Expected Contribution: A practical, replicable framework with quantitative results tailored to Indian multi-cloud deployments and DPDPA compliance. It directly addresses the visibility/maturity gap with actionable metrics and guidelines.
Feasibility: High. You can run the entire simulation on a laptop or free cloud tier. Tools are open-source and scriptable. Good entry point for IEEE CloudCom conference first, then journal expansion.

while true; do
  TS=$(date +%Y%m%d-%H%M%S)
  echo "=== [$TS] DPDPA Third-Party Risk Scan ==="

  # 1. SBOM Generation (vendor exchange simulation)
  for DEP in nginx-vulnerable nginx-secure; do
    POD=$(kubectl get pods -l app="$DEP" -o jsonpath='{.items[0].metadata.name}')
    IMAGE=$(kubectl get pod "$POD" -o jsonpath='{.spec.containers[0].image}')

    echo "Scanning $DEP ($IMAGE)..."
    syft "$IMAGE" -o json > "sbom-$DEP-$TS.json"

    # 2. Vulnerability Assessment
    grype sbom:"sbom-$DEP-$TS.json" > "grype-$DEP-$TS.txt"

    # 3. DPDPA Triggers
    if grep -q "HIGH\|CRITICAL" "grype-$DEP-$TS.txt"; then
      echo "🚨 DPDPA AUDIT TRIGGER: $DEP - High risk detected!"
      # trigger_audit.sh "$DEP" "HIGH_VULN"
    fi
  done

  sleep 300  # 5min continuous cycle
done
EOF
